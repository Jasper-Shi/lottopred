# V7 primary-source basis: post-RNG main/bonus role bias

Date: 2026-08-16

Research status: source review only; no historical outcomes were scored
Proposed slug: `v7_main_bonus_role_bias`

## Scope and conclusion

This note asks which mechanical or sampling-bias hypothesis is the most
defensible next *falsifiable* candidate for LOTTO 6/49, without selecting a
formula from the project's observed historical results. It deliberately does
not inspect V5/V6 numerical results and does not run a backtest.

The recommended single hypothesis is:

> **H7: In the current RNG draw regime, number identity has a stable effect on
> whether an already-selected number is assigned to one of the six main roles
> rather than the bonus role.**

This is a narrow sampling-stage hypothesis, not a claim that the lottery is
predictable. The fair and independently audited draw is the default
explanation. Under that default, number labels are exchangeable across the
seven roles, and the proposed feature should have no prospective skill.

The reason to test this hypothesis, despite its low prior probability, is that
it is tied to a documented output boundary, uses information not contained in
ordinary main-number frequency alone, has a targeted conditional negative
control, and can be rejected without searching windows, residue classes,
weights, or model parameters.

## Facts established by primary sources

1. The current ILC game conditions say that a Classic Draw consists of six
   main numbers and one bonus number, all seven different, drawn at random from
   1 through 49. They also distinguish main matches from a match involving the
   bonus number. This makes the main/bonus role a real, observable part of the
   official outcome, not a feature invented from the data. See the
   [ILC LOTTO 6/49 Game Conditions published by BCLC](https://corporate.bclc.com/content/dam/bclccorporate/documents/terms-and-conditions/rules-and-regulations/lotto/lotto-649-game-conditions.pdf),
   sections 4 and 5.

2. LOTTO 6/49 changed draw mechanisms in May 2019. Atlantic Lottery's official
   transition notice states specifically that the 2019-05-15 LOTTO 6/49
   winning numbers would be generated by RNG software. Its integrity page also
   describes the replacement of mechanical ball-drop machines by RNG software.
   See the [Atlantic Lottery transition notice](https://www.alc.ca/content/dam/alc/docs-en/Corp/AboutAL/Retailers/LMx2_Sellsheet_EN.PDF)
   and [Atlantic Lottery integrity statement](https://www.alc.ca/content/alc/en/corporate/about-atlantic-lottery/integrity-and-compliance.html).
   Therefore physical-ball history and current RNG history are different
   mechanism regimes and must not be pooled as though they were stationary.

3. Current national draws use RNG software. BCLC states that ILC national draws
   are conducted in Toronto, are audited by an independent firm, and use RNG
   software tested and certified for randomness, with auditors in separate
   geographical locations. OLG further states that the ILC system uses a
   proprietary algorithm, that GLI reviewed the software, and that independent
   auditors witness and verify the process. See
   [BCLC's official draw-process description](https://corporate.bclc.com/our-business/our-products/lottery/how-are-lottery-numbers-drawn-.html)
   and the [OLG LOTTO 6/49 RNG FAQ](https://www.olg.ca/en/frequently-asked-questions/lottery-games/lotto-649.html).

4. GLI's own published RNG standard calls for source review of randomness,
   scaling, and shuffling algorithms, and statistical analysis of final output
   for the intended distribution and independence both between draws and,
   where applicable, within a draw. This is useful context for why the null
   deserves strong prior weight; it is **not** evidence that every clause of
   that public generic standard was the exact engagement specification for the
   ILC certification. See
   [GLI-11 v3.0, chapter 3](https://gaminglabs.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/GLI-11-Gaming-Devices-V3-0.pdf)
   and [GLI's RNG testing submission requirements](https://gaminglabs.com/getting-started/technical-specifications-for-rng-testing/).

5. Lottery observations within a draw are dependent because sampling is
   without replacement. Johnson and Klotz developed likelihood methods for
   unequal number probabilities under sequential sampling without replacement;
   Joe derived uniformity tests for sets of lotto numbers and explained why the
   ordinary independent-cell chi-square formula is not appropriate. These
   papers support using a lottery-specific conditional/randomization test rather
   than treating the six or seven outputs as independent categorical draws:
   [Johnson and Klotz (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1993.10476320)
   and [Joe (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-7152(93)90141-5).

6. Coronel-Brizio et al. derived theoretical moments for lotto `k/N` outcomes
   and framed comparison with the hypergeometric model as *statistical
   auditing*. They explicitly note that the method can be adapted to test
   pseudorandom generators. That framing is important: a historical departure
   is an audit finding, not automatically a forecast. See
   [the original paper and preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.4595) and its
   [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2008.07.017).

7. Selecting an unusually high or low number after looking at a frequency chart
   is a multiple-comparison/data-snooping error. Hassler's original analysis of
   German 6/49 data makes the lottery-specific version of this point. It
   supports fixing the feature family and test before reading its result:
   [Hassler (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1111/insr.12488).

## Null, alternative, and mechanism interpretation

Let `M_d` be the set of six main numbers and `B_d` the one bonus number for draw
`d`. Under the fair null, for every label `i` in `1..49`:

```text
P(i is main) = 6 / 49
P(i is bonus) = 1 / 49
P(i is main | i is among the seven selected numbers) = 6 / 7
main:bonus conditional odds = 6:1
```

The registered alternative is only that the last conditional odds contain a
stable number-specific component in the post-transition RNG regime. A possible
cause could be a role-order, scaling, shuffling, or output-mapping imperfection.
The public documents do not expose the proprietary implementation, so a
departure could also come from upstream data labelling. Outcome data alone
cannot identify the cause, prove malicious manipulation, or distinguish a
software issue from a publication-pipeline issue.

This is more specific than a generic "hot number" claim. Conditioning on the
number already appearing among the seven deliberately removes total selection
frequency from the feature. It asks whether the *role assignment* is
exchangeable.

## The one fixed feature family

Use the name `rng_main_bonus_role_odds`. For target draw `t`, define the visible
history as all verified LOTTO 6/49 draws `d` satisfying:

```text
2019-05-15 <= draw_date(d) < target_date(t)
```

Do not use a rolling window and do not include pre-RNG draws. For each number
`i`, compute:

```text
m_i(t) = count of prior draws in which i was one of the six main numbers
b_i(t) = count of prior draws in which i was the bonus number

u_i(t) = log((m_i(t) + 3) / (b_i(t) + 1/2)) - log(6)
```

The half-counts are fixed before seeing results. They arise by assigning the
Jeffreys half-count to each of the seven exchangeable roles and then aggregating
the six main roles: `6 * 1/2 = 3` for main and `1 * 1/2` for bonus. The invariant
prior construction originates in
[Jeffreys (1946)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1946.0056). When no observations
exist, or when the smoothed main:bonus ratio is exactly 6:1, `u_i = 0`.

If a complete probability model is required, use no fitted coefficient. Set

```text
p_i(t) = logistic(alpha_t + u_i(t))
```

where `alpha_t` is the unique deterministic intercept satisfying
`sum_i p_i(t) = 6`. This guarantees `0 < p_i < 1`, gives an expected total of
six, and reduces exactly to the fair `6/49` vector when all `u_i` are zero.
There is no selectable scale, lag, decay, constraint, subgroup, or ensemble
weight in this definition.

### Bonus boundary

Historical bonus values strictly before `t` are an explicitly registered input
to this V7 feature. The target draw's bonus value is unavailable and prohibited
from the feature. The forecast target and every hit/rank/proper-score evaluation
remain the six main numbers only: matching the target bonus must never count as
a hit. A revealed target bonus may be used only in a separately pre-registered
aggregate role-exchangeability audit, never in its own prediction or predictive
score. Thus "bonus exclusion" applies to target prediction and scoring, not to
this clearly disclosed lagged predictor or post-reveal aggregate audit.

## Targeted negative control

The negative control should preserve everything except the proposed role
association:

1. Within each prior draw, hold its observed seven-number set and date fixed.
2. Uniformly select one of those seven labels as a pseudo-bonus; assign the
   other six as pseudo-main.
3. Process draws in chronological order with a frozen deterministic generator
   and seed `649`.
4. Recompute the identical `rng_main_bonus_role_odds` pipeline from these
   pseudo-roles.

This control preserves each draw's number set, all marginal seven-number
frequencies, chronology, regime coverage, and within-draw dependence. It
destroys only a stable association between number identity and main/bonus role.
It is consequently more diagnostic for H7 than shuffling whole draw dates.

For a global historical audit statistic, calibrate against within-draw role
permutations rather than an independent-cell chi-square reference. Freeze the
statistic, the number of Monte Carlo replicates (recommended: `10,000`), and the
seed before producing the audit result. Do not inspect 49 per-number effects and
then nominate whichever label looks best.

## Leakage and cohort requirements

- At target `t`, both real and control counts must stop strictly before `t`.
- The 2019-05-15 boundary is taken from the official transition notice, not
  selected from outcome performance.
- The pre-2019 mechanical-ball period may be reported only as a separately
  labelled mechanism diagnostic. It must not be pooled into the current-RNG
  feature and cannot validate a future-RNG forecast.
- The already observed 2020-2025 interval is consumed historical diagnostic
  evidence. It may reject H7, but a positive result cannot be called blind or
  confirmatory.
- Any observed 2026+ outcome is consumed for a version changed in response to
  it. A changed version needs a new identifier and a new prospective cohort.
- Predictions used as prospective evidence must be immutable, committed before
  the draw, and evaluated without the target bonus.

## Detection is not prediction

There are three different claims, and only the first can be addressed by an
ordinary historical audit:

1. **Detection:** past role labels depart from the fair conditional 6:1 pattern.
2. **Stability:** the same signed role effects persist after they were estimated.
3. **Prediction:** pre-draw probabilities improve outcomes for future main
   numbers versus the fair `6/49` forecast.

A small historical randomization p-value establishes neither stability nor
prediction. Conversely, a negative audit is a successful result: it supports
the documented fair-RNG explanation and closes this candidate without tuning.

Prediction must be judged prequentially—each probability precedes the outcome,
as in [Dawid's original prequential formulation](https://doi.org/10.2307/2981683)—and
with a proper probability score against the constant fair baseline. Brier's
original paper provides the squared-probability score
([Brier 1950](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/78/1/1520-0493_1950_078_0001_vofeit_2_0_co_2.xml));
Good's original decision-theoretic paper motivates logarithmic probability
scoring ([Good 1952](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1952.tb00104.x)). The
experiment registry must choose its one primary predictive metric before the
historical report is generated. Ranking hit counts may remain secondary, but
must not rescue worse proper-score performance post hoc.

No historical positive result should activate V7 in production. Shadow
activation, if the project permits it at all, must be a separately reviewed
decision; promotion requires the project's frozen prospective stopping rule.

## Why not the tempting alternatives?

- **Ball weight, wear, ink, or digit-shape effects:** mechanically plausible in
  the old ball-machine era, but there is no public draw-level ball-set/machine
  metadata here and the mechanism was replaced in 2019. Such a result would not
  be a current-RNG predictor.
- **A hand-picked modulo or bit-width pattern:** the ILC algorithm is
  proprietary. Choosing residues, bit widths, or number partitions without an
  implementation specification would be unconstrained pattern search.
- **A discovered hot/cold number:** selecting labels or windows after seeing
  their counts is precisely the snooping problem the preregistration is meant
  to prevent.
- **Day-of-week effects:** official sources establish Wednesday and Saturday
  draws, but do not establish distinct RNG implementations by weekday. Adding
  that interaction would introduce another unsupported feature family.

The main/bonus role feature is therefore the narrowest publicly observable
mechanism probe with a clean conditional control. Its expected outcome remains
negative. That expectation is a reason to pre-register a decisive falsification
test, not a reason to search for a more favourable formula.
